# Statements

How a `Statement`, its `Proof` and a `Ref` behave on the page, and what each of their failures means. They fail under [Components](rules.md#components), as `statement-id`, `statement-section`, `statement-solution`, `citation-target` and `proof-target`.

- **Statements stand at a section's top level.** A statement's number is the next clause number, in the paragraphs' series, and the margin numbers only a section's own paragraphs, `###`s and statements, so a statement anywhere else would have no number to cite. "the statement `x` has no section number" (`statement-section`) means it comes before the first `##` heading, or sits inside a list, a question or another component. Move it to the top level of a section.
- **Statement IDs:** readable lowercase kebab-case (`valor-medio`), unique among the artifact's statements, written as a plain string, `id="…"`. A question may use the same ID. A statement's ID holds no answers, so renaming it only means updating its citations. Failures read "the statement ID `x` is already used at line N …" or "… isn't lowercase kebab-case".
- **Citations:** cite a statement anywhere in the artifact with `<Ref to="…" />`, which reads "Teorema 2.1" and links to it; write the article yourself ("pelo <Ref to="valor-medio" />"). "the citation `x` names no statement in this artifact" (`citation-target`) means no `Statement` has the `id` that `Ref`'s `to` names. Correct `to`, or reword the passage when the statement is gone.
- **Proofs:** write the proof the source gives a theorem, lemma or corollary as a `<Proof of="…">` right after the statement: it and its paragraphs take no number, and the page opens it with "Demonstração." and ends it with a square. Keep it at the section's top level too: nested in a list or a question, a proof never counts as following its statement, so it always opens by naming it, and its equations go unnumbered. A proof the source gives later opens "Demonstração do Teorema 2.1." on its own, so keep it where the source puts it.
- **Proofs prove claims:** a `Proof`'s `of` names the `id` of a theorem, lemma or corollary. "the proof of `x` names no statement in this artifact" (`proof-target`) means no `Statement` has that `id`: correct `of`. "… names a statement of kind `definition`" (or `example`) means it proves something that takes no proof: point `of` at the result it proves, or make the text a paragraph (for an example, its solution after `---`).
- **Examples:** a `<Statement kind="example">` holds the problem, `---` between blank lines, then the solution, which the page shows openly after "Solução.".
- **Only examples have solutions:** "the example `x` has no solution" (`statement-solution`) means it lacks a `---` between blank lines with the solution after it: write both. A `---` right under a line of text turns that line into a heading, so keep the blank line above it. "the `theorem` `x` has a `---`, but only an example has a solution" means a definition, theorem, lemma or corollary holds one: make it `kind="example"` when it works a problem, or move what follows the `---` after the statement, as a `<Proof of="x">` when it proves a theorem, lemma or corollary and as a paragraph otherwise. "… has a `---` with nothing after it" means the `---` is stray: remove it.
