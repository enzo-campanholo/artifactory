## The formula

<SingleChoice id={questionIds.singleChoice} answer={2}>

Which expression gives the area of a circle of radius $r$?

1. $2 \pi r$
2. $\pi r^2$
3. $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$

---

An area is a length times a length, so its formula carries $r^2$; only b) does. a) is the circumference, a length, and c) is the volume of a ball of radius $r$, a length cubed. Scaling the radius by $k$ scales the area by $k^2$:

$$
\pi (k r)^2 = k^2 \cdot \pi r^2
$$

  caption="Twice the radius covers four squares of side r where the first covered one."
/>

</SingleChoice>

  answer={numericKey.value}
  tolerance={numericKey.tolerance}
  unit="cm²"
>

What is the area of a circle of radius $3\,\mathrm{cm}$, to two decimal places?

<Figure
  src={radiusThree}
  alt="A shaded disc with its centre marked and a radius of 3 cm drawn from it."
  caption="The circle, of radius 3 cm."
/>

---

Square the radius first, then multiply by $\pi$:

$$
\pi r^2 = \pi \cdot (3\,\mathrm{cm})^2 = 9 \pi\,\mathrm{cm}^2 \approx 28.27\,\mathrm{cm}^2
$$

<Diagram
  src={areaSteps}
  alt="The radius, 3 cm, is squared to 9 square centimetres, which times pi gives about 28.27 square centimetres."
  caption="The order of the two steps."
/>

Squaring after multiplying, $(3 \pi)^2 \approx 88.83$, gives the area of a circle of radius $3 \pi\,\mathrm{cm}$ instead.

</NumericQuestion>

<Matching answers={matchingAnswers}>

What does each formula give, for a radius $r$?

1. The circumference of a circle
2. The area of a circle
3. The surface area of a ball
4. The volume of a ball

---

- $2 \pi r$
- $\pi r^2$
- $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$

---

Count the powers of $r$: a length carries $r$, an area $r^2$ and a volume $r^3$. So $2 \pi r$ is the circumference and $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$ the volume of the ball. The disc and the ball's surface are both areas, and the factor tells them apart:

- the disc's area is $\pi r^2$;
- the ball's surface is four discs of its radius, $4 \pi r^2$, so c) is right for no item.

## Where it comes from

<OpenQuestion id={questionIds.openQuestion}>

Why is the area of a circle $\pi r^2$?

---

Cut the disc into thin rings. The ring at radius $\rho$ has length $2 \pi \rho$ and width $\mathrm{d}\rho$, so the area adds up to $\int_0^r 2 \pi \rho \, \mathrm{d}\rho = \pi r^2$.

<Figure
  src={rings}
  alt="A disc cut into concentric rings, with one ring at radius rho drawn thick."
  caption="The ring at radius ρ."
/>

</OpenQuestion>

Unrolled, the rings stack into a triangle of base $2 \pi r$ and height $r$, whose area is $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \pi r \cdot r = \pi r^2$:

<Figure
  src={unrolled}
  alt="A right triangle of base 2 pi r and height r, shaded, with one thin strip across it at mid height."
  caption="The rings unrolled into a triangle."
/>

## What to remember

The area grows with the square of the radius: double the radius and the area is four times as large.
